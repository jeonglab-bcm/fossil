The Diabetes Classifier, dug back up.

Neither the web tool nor its code survived. We rebuilt the cohort from the NHANES 1999 to 2004 files and the method from the text. Diagnosed diabetes comes back at 1,266 people (paper 1,266). Over 10 random splits the SVM’s median test AUC is 0.825 for diabetes and 0.732 for pre-diabetes or undiagnosed diabetes (paper 0.8347 and 0.7318), and it ties with logistic regression, as the paper says.

The paper’s own numbers: 14 of 15 arithmetic checks on Tables 1 and 3 hold. Our code against R: On split 0: logistic coefficients equal to survey::svyglm within 6.9e-12; DeLong P equal to pROC::roc.test within 1.4e-07; SVM test AUC from e1071 (LIBSVM in R) with the same C and gamma 0.8187 against 0.8187, 0.7481 against 0.7481.

The main claim holds: on both schemes the SVM discriminates as well as the logistic regression, and the DeLong test finds no difference in 8 and 8 of 10 splits. The polynomial kernels come back better than published, as an untuned model would explain; the scheme I 10-fold CV and test PPV stay apart, cause open.

What came back

The cohort
All 33 NHANES files still download from the CDC. Diagnosed diabetes at 1,266 people; the scheme I totals at 1,462 cases and 4,843 non-cases (paper 1,461 and 4,853).
The method
LIBSVM with grid.py’s default grid, trained on all cases and as many random non-cases. It is now a Python package, diabclass, with a tutorial: PMC2850872/package.
The comparison
A survey-weighted logistic regression and the DeLong test, checked against R’s survey and pROC packages.
The tie
On scheme I the SVM and the logistic regression differ by -0.0000 in median AUC; on scheme II by -0.0018. DeLong P is below 0.05 in 2 and 2 of 10 splits.

What stayed buried

  • The Diabetes Classifier web tool and its Java code
  • The paper’s random 80/20 split and its undersampled training set
  • Which NHANES items stand for family history, hypertension and physical activity, and the divisors for weight, height, waist and BMI
  • The polynomial kernel’s degree and offset, and the survey weight given to SUDAAN

The main claim holds: on both schemes the SVM discriminates as well as the logistic regression, and the DeLong test finds no difference in 8 and 8 of 10 splits. The polynomial kernels come back better than published, as an untuned model would explain; the scheme I 10-fold CV and test PPV stay apart, cause open.
