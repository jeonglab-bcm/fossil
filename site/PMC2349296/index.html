Co-occurrence and the corpus statistics are arithmetic on the annotation, so where the corpus is the paper’s they come back exactly. AIMed’s surviving copy lacks 12 sentences and its self-interactions (about 5% of AIMed’s interactions in the paper), so its co-occurrence recall is 1.00, not 0.95. RelEx was never released; ours is rebuilt from the two papers’ descriptions. Its F is within 7 points of the paper’s on the three matching corpora, but it matches 0 of 9 of their RelEx cells.

The claims in the text

The paper’s summary numbers. Ours is our run: it is the reproduction, and the verdict reads only that column. Paper’s tables runs the same code on the paper’s own Tables 2 and 4; it returns the paper’s text, which shows we compute each number the way the paper did, not that our run agrees. Paper’s BioInfer row is our run with BioInfer’s numbers replaced by the paper’s: it shows how much BioInfer matters, and moves toward the paper by construction.

The main claim holds: in our run RelEx’s F varies by 19.5 points between corpora, more than the 8.2-point gap between the methods. The numbers that differ have two causes. BioInfer’s surviving copy is a later conversion, and it sets our smallest error cut (−2.3%, BioInfer). Our rebuild of RelEx is the other cause: on HPRD50, IEPA and LLL, whose corpora are the paper’s, it matches 0 of 9 of the paper’s RelEx cells in Table 2. On IEPA, co-occurrence F is the paper’s 0.58, but RelEx F is 0.60 against 0.67. So even with the paper’s BioInfer row the smallest error cut is 4.1% (IEPA) against 21%, and the gap between the methods is 11.5 points against 13. With the paper’s BioInfer row, the filtered difference (10.1 points) and the Kendall p (0.083) come near the paper’s, but that column takes one corpus from the paper; our run gives 15.7 points and 0.233.

What came back

The corpora
The unified XML of all five corpora survives only in a third-party GitHub copy (metalrt/ppi-dataset, commit dd49ea3). HPRD50, IEPA and LLL match the paper; AIMed has 1943 of 1,955 sentences; BioInfer is the later, improved binarization with 2,534 interactions.
The tokenizer
MedPost’s tokens give Table 3’s token counts exactly on all five corpora, so the paper counted MedPost tokens.
Stanford Parser 1.5.1
The 2006 release the paper names is still on Stanford’s download server and runs on Java 11. Every sentence parses.
RelEx’s relation terms
Fundel et al.’s supplementary list, recovered from a 2014 Wayback capture of their page: 175 stems, which expand to the 1,048 terms their paper reports.
RelEx
Rules 1 to 3, enumeration expansion, the negation filter and the term filter, rebuilt in Python on the 1.5.1 dependencies. Developed on HPRD50 only, then run once on all corpora.
MedPost on 64-bit
The 2008 source counts its abbreviation table in bytes, not entries, and crashes on x86_64. One line fixes it.

What stayed buried

  • The paper’s BioInfer conversion (Causal-Change interactions only). The archived download page still links it, but the file itself was never archived
  • The conversion programs at mars.cs.utu.fi/PPICorpora
  • The authors’ RelEx code, and the details of its chunking and negation list
  • The 250 interactions behind Fig. 3 and Table 5, and their manual labels. Fig. 4 is an illustration

What we matched

Figs 1 and 2 are base R plots. We draw them with base R on the published pixel grid: the plot region comes from the tick marks of the published axes, so a data point lands where the paper’s would. Only the text size, symbol size and legend spacing are fitted, on a render of the published values.

Our run
The five unified corpora from the only surviving copy, MedPost for tokens and tags, Stanford Parser 1.5.1 (the version the paper names) for dependencies, and our reimplementation of RelEx with Fundel et al.’s own 1,048 relation terms.
Axes
One linear map per axis from the tick marks (x = 1 to 5, y = 0 to 100), plus R’s 4% axis extension and half a pixel for the pixel centre.
Fitted, not data
Point size 12 (R’s default), default label distances, symbol size 1.2, legend symbol spacing 1.3 to 1.4. Fitted on a render of the paper’s own Table 2 and 4 values.
Two renders per figure
“Our run” draws our F-scores. “Paper’s values” draws the published table values with the same code, to show how much of the difference is drawing and how much is data.
Reading the views
Overlay: ink is the paper only, light green is the reproduction only, dark green is both. Difference: darker means a larger grey-level difference (×2).
