Exposure-specific risk, dug back up.

Reichmann and colleagues ran their simulation in SAS and published it in 2011 without the code. In November 2026 we rebuilt it from the paper and reran all 119 settings. Of 36 results, 24 agree, 9 partly agree and 1 differs, 13 of these verdicts set in review. Every gap has a tested cause.

What stayed buried

  • The SAS programs. The paper names SAS 9.2 and released nothing; no copy was found.
  • The log interval of ESR_R in the simulation. Table S6 and the ESR_R bars of Figs 1 to 4 behave as if the first term of eq. 10 were left out; with it, as printed, our intervals cover more often. The case study used eq. 10 as printed.

What we matched

What the paper does not say, and what we chose. Each choice was tested against the paper's tables.

True ESR
RR times the risk in the unexposed, unrounded (0.135, not the label .14). The rounded label misses many cells of Tables S1 and S2.
Zero cells
With no cases in one group of sample 2, the log interval is (0, infinity). Leaving those pairs out lowers coverage in Scenario 1; counting them as misses or adding 0.5 misses Tables S5 and S7.
Population
10 million people per setting, both samples drawn without replacement. Fixed proportions instead of random draws change nothing.
Tolerance
Half a printed unit plus three combined Monte Carlo standard errors, fixed before the comparison.
