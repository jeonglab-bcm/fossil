miRNA enrichment bias, dug back up.

A test that called random miRNA lists enriched, and a fix that called almost nothing enriched. We regenerated the paper’s miRanda predictions and rebuilt its empirical test. 99.87% of the paper’s published target lists come back, Figure 1 lands on the published bars, and after correction 6 of 22 human and 0 of 7 mouse lists have an enriched term (paper: 5 and 0).

What came back

The GO universe
Ensembl 75’s BioMart tables survive as MySQL dumps. By gene name, direct annotations, all evidence codes, they give the paper’s 15,733 human genes and all 9,462 of S1’s term sizes.
The miRanda thresholds
“Score > 155, energy < −20” is applied to miRanda’s per-UTR totals, inclusive (score ≥ 155, energy ≤ −20), on a run with miRanda’s defaults. Per-site filtering finds a third of S3’s genes; a strict > 155 misses about 3%.
The 3′ UTRs
Cut from the Ensembl 75 GTF and genome; they equal BioMart’s 3′ UTRs for all 104,763 coding transcripts. Haplotype and patch sequences need their shared regions copied from the parent chromosome.
The empirical P-value
S5’s P-values are counts over 999,999 draws with no pseudo-count, the overlap divides by all target genes (S5’s 1,064 include genes without a GO term), and BH runs over all 9,462 terms.
S5’s eight miRNAs
Not the eight with the most targets in the term, as the text says: S5 rules out every miRNA that hits a term it reports as empty, and exactly eight survive.
A spreadsheet in the way
S3 lists MARCH1, MARCH5, SEPT7 and SEP15 as dates (36951.0 is 1 March 2001).

What stayed buried

  • The authors’ Python script (sgjlab.org/empirical-go no longer resolves)
  • Table 3 (rice): psRNATarget’s 2011 server and the agriGO tables it used
  • The robustness runs of Section 3.3: PITA v6, DIANA-microT-v4, starBase v2 CLIP sets and DAVID’s 2014 BP_FAT
  • How the multi-hit algorithm counts sites on a gene with several transcripts
  • Why 200 of S3’s 155,795 study–gene pairs are not in ours: the genes have short 3′ UTRs here, and no Ensembl 75 table we found gives them longer ones

The bias the paper reports comes back: with regenerated predictions, the mean ion-transport count of 39 random miRNAs is 250.6, and the hypergeometric test calls it significant at 5.97e-07. Corrected, almost nothing is enriched.

What we matched

Figure 1 is a base R plot: two histograms of probability per 5-gene bin. We draw it with base R on the published pixel grid. The plot region comes from the tick marks of the published axes, so a bar lands where the paper’s would. Only the raster resolution, the y-title and legend positions and the fill colours are fitted, on a render of the bar heights read off the published figure.

Our run
miRanda 3.3a (bioconda build) on all 2,578 human and 1,908 mouse miRBase 20 mature miRNAs against every Ensembl 75 3′ UTR; the empirical null rebuilt from the Methods and checked against S1, S3 and S5.
Axes
One linear map per axis from the tick marks (x = 150 to 300, y = 0 to 0.20), R’s 4% axis extension, and half a pixel for the pixel centre.
Fitted, not data
Raster resolution 118 dpi at R’s default 12 pt, Liberation Sans for Helvetica, the y-title centre and the legend position, and the two fill colours (sampled from the JPEG).
Two renders
“Our run” draws our null. “Paper’s values” draws the bar heights read off the published figure with the same code.
Reading the views
Overlay: ink is the paper only, light green is the reproduction only, dark green is both. Difference: darker means a larger grey-level difference (×2).

The paper’s main claim holds on regenerated predictions: the standard hypergeometric test flags terms that random miRNAs hit just as often, and the empirical test leaves most published lists with nothing.
