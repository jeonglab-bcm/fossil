Odds ratios that run high in small samples, dug back up.

We ran the study again: 281 sample sizes, 1,000 samples each, 281,000 logistic fits, and 20 more populations to see how far a rerun moves. Of 19 results, 5 came back exactly and 2 agree; 3 partly agree and 7 differ.

The main claim holds: in our run logistic regression pushes both coefficients away from zero, more at small n, and the sampling distribution is skewed at small n and grows symmetric. Table 1's slopes, SEs and R squared values and the figures' spreads do not come back; the causes are open (the paper's per-size means scatter less than any population of Additional file 1's design gives), and the intercepts move with the population draw.

What came back

The direction of the bias
Away from zero for both exposures, at every size.
The shape
Skewed at n = 100, near symmetric at n = 1000; fewer extreme estimates as n grows.
The intercepts
2.011 and -0.898 lie inside our 20 populations.
The population
Additional file 1's R lines run unchanged, and P(x>2.5) = 0.2375.

What stayed buried

  • Table 1's slopes, SEs and R squared values: our means scatter more than the paper's.
  • The spreads of Figs 2 and 3: about 1.5 times wider in our run.
  • How the paper handled separated samples, and its seed.
