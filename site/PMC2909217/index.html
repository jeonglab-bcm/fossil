What came back

Data
The Tong 2004 screen, BioGRID interactions and Gavin and Ho complexes from SGD's copy of 2009-12-19; 48129 complex edges against about 49,000; 4747 deletion-array genes against about 4,700.
Method
Divisive correlation clustering, eq. 2 weights, the 10 properties and 18 features, label spreading and an RBF SVM, as the package fgnssl.
Weighted against binary
SSL area under the ROC curve 0.664 on the weighted network, 0.656 on the binary one, as the paper orders them.
SGI pairs sit higher
SGI pairs have the higher average degree and closeness, as the Discussion says.

What stayed buried

  • The ROC curves. SSL reaches 0.675 to 0.645 under the curve and SVM 0.705 to 0.678; the paper's curves enclose about 0.95.
  • SSL over SVM. SVM leads at every training size on our run.
  • Table 1 and Fig. 6. Our KS statistics differ; the paper's P-values imply about 5,531 pairs per test, ours 3,029.
  • Eisen's 1998 heat-shock time course; the paper's protein-interaction filter, SSL settings, SVM grid and seeds.

What we matched

What we fixed to make the run, and where it came from.

Protein interactions
Two-hybrid and Affinity Capture-Western, 13007 interactions (paper 12,990), the closest interpretable filter.
Expression
54 conditions from SGD's processed sets (paper 64).
Non-SGI pairs
Tong query genes by the non-essential deletion array, 586472 tested pairs.
SSL
alpha 0.99, sigma chosen on the training labels.
SVM
RBF kernel, C and gamma by grid search on the training folds.
