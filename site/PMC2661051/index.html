The papersThe code

Gene set modules, dug back up.

The authors' R code is lost. We rebuilt every module from the paper as the R package gseamod, reran the simulation study (100 data sets) and redid the p53 and Hedenfalk analyses. Of 42 results we tried, 19 came back within their tolerance as printed, 7 agree, 8 partly and 8 differ.

R package
install.packages("remotes")
remotes::install_github("jeonglab-bcm/fossil/PMC2661051/package")

It needs R 4.3 or later and the Bioconductor package limma (installed by remotes from the biocViews field), fdrtool and corpcor from CRAN, and, for the globaltest track only, Bioconductor's globaltest.

What came back

The modules
Three gene-level statistics, five transformations, six gene set statistics, four significance assessments, globaltest and Hotelling, as the R package gseamod.
The simulation
206 of 207 rates of Tables 2, 4 and 5 within Monte Carlo error; 51 of 54 in Table 3 and 25 of 27 in Table 6.
The p53 data
The paper's filter on GSEA's collapsed file gives exactly 8,768 genes and 290 sets of 15 to 321 genes.

Hotelling, globaltest, ES and maxmean within one set of the published bars at cutoff 0.01.
Table 7
breast_cancer_estrogen_signalling first again, and 27 of the 41 sets at their printed p.

What stayed buried

  • The paper's cond. FDR statistic: neither of two readings of it gives its Table 3 numbers, and the mean-of-fdr reading also misses Fig. 2.
  • Its Hotelling correlation estimate: ours finds the correlated set 1 more often.
  • Its Hedenfalk gene mapping and its 89 sets: ours has 2,233 genes and 84 sets, and finds more sets.
  • The data behind Fig. 3: our within-set correlations run a little lower.
