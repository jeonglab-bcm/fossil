Sample size for multilevel logistic regression, dug back up.

Moineddin, Matheson and Glazier ran their simulation in SAS with PROC NLMIXED and published it in 2007 without the code. In November 2026 we rebuilt the data and an NLMIXED-like fit from the paper and reran every design: 27 cells of 1,000 data sets for Tables 1 to 3, 27,000 more for Table 4 and 7,200 for Table 5. 1315 of the 1395 printed cells came back within Monte Carlo error. Of 28 results, 6 agree, 20 partly agree and 1 were not attempted. Every gap has a tested cause.

Convergence
The pattern came back: fits fail with few small groups and a small random intercept. Our NLMIXED-like fit converges in 54.7% to 100.0% of data sets per design, against the paper's 56% to 100%.
Prevalence and non-normal data

The method
mlagq, a Python package that fits multilevel logistic regression by adaptive Gauss-Hermite quadrature the way NLMIXED does, with a tutorial. Its likelihood equals a direct numerical integral.

What stayed buried

  • The SAS programs. The paper names SAS 9.1 and PROC NLMIXED and released no code; SAS cannot be run here.
  • Which fits NLMIXED called converged. That verdict decides the group-size-5 rows of Tables 1 and 3 and Table 5's low-convergence models; our optimizer keeps a different set of fits.
  • Some numbers of the text. A few do not follow from the paper's own tables.
