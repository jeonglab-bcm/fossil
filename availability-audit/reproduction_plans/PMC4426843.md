## 1. Paper

| Field | Value |

| Title | Bias in microRNA functional enrichment analysis |
| Authors | Thomas Bleazard, Janine A Lamb, Sam Griffiths-Jones |
| Journal | Bioinformatics 31(10):1592–1598 |
| Year | 2015 |
| PMCID | PMC4426843 |
| DOI | 10.1093/bioinformatics/btv023 |
| iCite RCR | 3.046 (paper_catalog.csv; 96 citations, NIH percentile 84.2) |

**Summary.** The "standard method" of miRNA functional enrichment works in three steps:
1. take the union of predicted targets of a miRNA list;
2. annotate them with GO biological-process (BP) terms;
3. test each term with the hypergeometric distribution against all GO-annotated genes.

The paper shows that this null is wrong for miRNA targets. Random miRNA sets give strongly "significant" terms because predicted targets are biased (for example, towards long 3′ UTRs). The proposed empirical test compares the observed GO-term overlap with overlaps from one million random miRNA sets of the same size. A multi-hit variant weights genes by their number of predicted sites. Predictions use miRanda 3.3a (score > 155, energy < −20 kcal/mol) for miRBase 20 mature miRNAs against Ensembl 75 human and mouse 3′ UTRs, with Ensembl 75 GO BP annotations. **Main claims:**
- For 39 random miRNAs, the mean "ion transport" target count (250) already gives hypergeometric P = 5.97×10⁻⁷ (Fig 1). Many common GO terms are biased (Table 1).
- After BH correction, only 5/22 human and 0/7 mouse published miRNA lists show any enriched GO term (Table 2), and 2/4 rice lists (Table 3).
- The result is robust to KEGG, intersection-based predictions and CLIP filtering (text and S2).

## 6. Risks and open questions

- **Prediction set fidelity.** Everything downstream depends on reproducing the miRanda target sets. S3 gives an exact per-list check (step 3). Mismatches would most likely come from the UTR transcript choice, or from miRanda build differences (e.g. a different Vienna RNA version for the energy calculation).
- **GO construction.** Propagation and evidence codes are unspecified. S1 term sizes allow a direct check.
- **Monte Carlo noise.** With 10⁶ draws, the standard error of an empirical P near 0.01 is about 10⁻⁴, and the smallest attainable P is about 10⁻⁶. Counts of significant terms near the BH threshold (e.g. Liu multi-hit = 1) can still flip; the large counts (thousands) should agree to within a few percent if the target sets match.
- **Rice pipeline.** It depends on web services that changed (psRNATarget V2 scoring, 2017) or could not be reached from here. Table 3 may end up "no".
- **Open question.** Was the 15,733-gene universe the Ensembl 75 BP set or a DAVID background? The text says the parameters were "chosen to mirror" DAVID. Step 2 resolves this.

**Verdict:** Fig 1 and Tables 1–2 are regenerable approximately in about 4–5 days (most of it miRanda and sampling compute). S1 and S3 give exact intermediate checks, and the hypergeometric part of Table 1 can be verified exactly within an hour. Table 3 (rice) is at best approximate and depends on reaching psRNATarget and agriGO.
