# Reproduction plan: PMC2349296

**Comparative analysis of five protein-protein interaction corpora**
Pyysalo S, Airola A, Heimonen J, Björne J, Ginter F, Salakoski T.
*BMC Bioinformatics* 9 (Suppl 3): S6, 2008 (LBM 2007 proceedings).
PMCID PMC2349296 · PMID 18426551 · DOI [10.1186/1471-2105-9-S3-S6](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-9-S3-S6) · iCite RCR 2.586 (92 citations).

**Summary.** The authors take five PPI corpora (AIMed, BioInfer, HPRD50, IEPA, LLL) and convert them to one shared annotation level: undirected, untyped binary interactions between gold entities, with no trigger words, no negations and no certainty. For BioInfer they also flatten nested entities, binarise complex interactions and keep only the Causal-Change subtree of the ontology. They then run two extraction methods on every corpus with gold named entities: a sentence co-occurrence baseline and their own reimplementation of RelEx, a dependency-parse rule system (MedPost tagger plus Stanford Lexicalized Parser 1.5.1). **Main claim:** the same method's F-score varies on average by 19 percentage points between corpora (in some cases by more than 30). That is more than the 13-point average gap between the naive and the advanced method. About half of the between-corpus gap comes from the annotation of entities that take part in no interaction (the ratio of interactions to entity pairs, I/EP). Removing those entities cuts the average pairwise RelEx difference from 19 to 11 points. Two annotators also analysed 50 random interactions per corpus (type, directness, explicitness, certainty, polarity).

## Reproduction plan

In parallel, try a network that can reach web.archive.org: if the 2008-09-13 capture of `mars.cs.utu.fi/PPICorpora` holds the original converted corpora, use those instead, since they would be the paper's exact version.
2. **Co-occurrence baseline** (deterministic, about 2 h after the loader). Score all entity pairs in each sentence against the gold interactions under the paper's three rules. Compare with Table 2:

   2. Tag with MedPost and parse with the oldest reachable Stanford parser, using SD collapsed dependencies.
   3. Implement dependency-based chunking, rules 1–3, and the negation and relation-term filters.
   4. Develop on HPRD50 only, which was RelEx's own test corpus.

## Risks and open questions

- **Corpus version.** The mirror is a later, third-party copy with unknown provenance. Its BioInfer is not the paper's Causal-Change-only conversion, and its AIMed is missing 12 sentences. Every BioInfer number and every "average pairwise difference" will move. Redoing the paper's BioInfer conversion needs the native BioInfer XML, which we could not find online.
- **RelEx is a reimplementation of a reimplementation.** The paper itself says its version "diverges slightly" from the original. Chunking, the term lists and the parser version are all unspecified or unobtainable. Expect several F-points of difference per corpus. The claims are about the ranking of corpora and the size of the gaps, so check those rather than the exact values.
- **Parser.** If Stanford 1.5.1 cannot be found, the dependency labels from 2.0.x could differ in collapsed `prep_*` and `conj_*` handling.
- **Open question.** Do the 2008 Wayback capture of `mars.cs.utu.fi/PPICorpora` and the 2008-era BioInfer site hold downloadable files? This needs checking from a network that can reach web.archive.org.
- **License.** The mirror states no license. The original corpora have their own terms, so check before redistributing any derived files.

**Verdict:** The co-occurrence parts of Tables 2–4, Figures 1–2 and the Table 1 size row can be regenerated approximately in about 1 day, and probably exactly for HPRD50, IEPA and LLL. The RelEx columns need a 1–2 week reimplementation and will be approximate. Figure 3 and Table 5 cannot be regenerated, and Figure 4 is an illustration.
