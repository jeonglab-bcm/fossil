## 1. Paper

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Title | Application of support vector machine modeling for prediction of common diseases: the case of diabetes and pre-diabetes |
| Authors | Wei Yu, Tiebin Liu, Rodolfo Valdez, Marta Gwinn, Muin J Khoury |
| Journal | BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making 10:16 |
| Year | 2010 |
| PMCID | PMC2850872 |
| DOI | 10.1186/1472-6947-10-16 |
| iCite RCR | 6.196 (paper_catalog.csv; 190 citations, NIH percentile 94.5) |

**Summary.** SVMs (LIBSVM, with C and γ tuned by `grid.py` under 5-fold CV) classify diabetes status of non-pregnant adults aged 20 and over in NHANES 1999–2004. Only simple non-laboratory variables are used. There are two schemes:
- **Scheme I:** diagnosed or undiagnosed diabetes vs everyone else. Eight variables (family history, age, race/ethnicity, weight, height, waist, BMI, hypertension); the RBF kernel is best.
- **Scheme II:** undiagnosed diabetes or pre-diabetes vs no diabetes. The same eight variables plus sex and physical activity; the linear kernel is best.

Models are trained on a random 80% with non-cases undersampled to match cases, and tested on the remaining 20%. **Main claim:** SVM discrimination (test AUC 0.8347 for scheme I and 0.7318 for scheme II) equals survey-weighted logistic regression (0.8319 and 0.7335; DeLong p = 0.3672 and 0.6718). The paper also built a demonstration web tool, "Diabetes Classifier".

## 2. Status

**Software: lost.** The web tool at http://www.hugenavigator.net/DiseaseClassificationPortal/startPageDiabetes.do is dead. The catalog places the paper in tier E (no Wayback capture), and no new home was found. The code was never released.

**Data: complete and public.** CDC still serves the NHANES public-use SAS XPT files. All 33 needed files answered HTTP 200 on 2026-11-02 (95.9 MB in total):

| Cycle | Files |
| --- | --- |
| 1999–2000 | DEMO, DIQ, BMX, BPQ, BPX, MCQ, PAQ, SMQ, ALQ, LAB10AM (fasting glucose), PH (fasting questionnaire) |
| 2001–2002 | the same with suffix _B (L10AM_B) |
| 2003–2004 | the same with suffix _C (L10AM_C) |

- **URLs.** The pattern is `https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/<year>/DataFiles/<FILE>.xpt`.
- **Versions.** Most files carry Last-Modified 2014-05-22 (re-hosting). **LAB10AM and L10AM_B were modified on 2016-03-10**, after publication, so the fasting-glucose files the authors used have since been revised.
- **Variables checked** in the downloaded subset: DIQ010 (DIQ), LBXGLU, WTSAF2YR and WTSAF4YR (fasting labs; 3,267 / 3,666 / 3,356 participants of all ages ≥ 12), PHAFSTHR and PHAFSTMN (PH), PAD020, PAQ100 and PAQ180 (PAQ), BPQ020 and BPQ040A (BPQ).
- **Not published:** the training/test splits and the undersampled non-case sets.

**Tools.**
- LIBSVM releases from that time (2.88, 2.89, 2.9) are available from csie.ntu.edu.tw/~cjlin/libsvm/oldfiles/; each includes `grid.py`.
- SUDAAN 9 (RTI) is commercial. Substitutes are R `survey::svyglm` or SAS PROC SURVEYLOGISTIC with the NHANES strata, PSU and weights.
- The DeLong test is available in R `pROC`.

## 6. Risks and open questions

- **Splits are unpublished.** Exact AUCs cannot be matched; the realistic target is "paper value inside our distribution over seeds" (likely ±0.01–0.02).
- **Variable definitions.** Family history, hypertension and physical activity are not mapped to NHANES items, and different choices shift scheme II most, since it is the weaker signal.
- **Data revisions.** The 1999–2002 fasting-lab files were re-released in 2016. If Table 1 counts differ by more than a few dozen, check the 2016 change notes before blaming the definitions.
- **SUDAAN substitute.** R survey should give the same point estimates of β. AUCs are then computed unweighted on the test set, as the paper appears to have done.
- **Open question.** The scheme II training PPV (0.6729) is far above its test PPV (0.5061). That is consistent with a balanced training set, so training-row metrics must be computed on the undersampled set, not the full 80%.

**Verdict:** Tables 1–3 and Fig 2 are regenerable approximately in about 3 days. The splits are unpublished and several variable mappings must be chosen, so expect agreement in distribution, not to four decimals. Fig 1 is a schematic.
