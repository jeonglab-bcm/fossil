| Field | Value |

| Title | Identifying DNA-binding proteins by combining support vector machine and PSSM distance transformation |
| Authors | Xu R, Zhou J, Wang H, He Y, Wang X, Liu B |
| Journal | BMC Systems Biology 9 (Suppl 1): S10 (APBC 2015) |
| Year | 2015 |
| PMCID / PMID | PMC4331676 / 25708928 |
| DOI | 10.1186/1752-0509-9-S1-S10 |
| iCite RCR | 2.438 (paper_catalog.csv) |

**Summary.** The paper classifies whole protein sequences as DNA-binding or not. Each sequence gets a PSI-BLAST PSSM against NCBI nr (3 iterations, E-value 0.001). The PSSM (L×20) is turned into a fixed-length vector by "PSSM distance transformation" (PSSM-DT). For each lag lg = 1..LG, PSSM-SDT averages the product of the PSSM scores of the same amino-acid column at positions j and j+lg (20 features per lag). PSSM-DDT does the same for each ordered pair of different columns (380 features per lag). With LG = 5 this gives 2,000 features, which go to a LIBSVM RBF classifier. The main claim is that SVM-PSSM-DT beats earlier PSSM encodings and earlier predictors. On a benchmark of 525 DNA-binding and 550 non-binding PDB chains (jackknife) it reports ACC 79.96%, MCC 0.622 and AUC 86.5%. On the independent PDB186 set it reports ACC 80.00%, MCC 0.647 and AUC 87.4%, against 76.9% / 0.538 / 79.1% for the best earlier method (DBPPred). A discriminant-weight analysis ranks the residue pairs (R,R), (R,P), (P,R) and (A,R) as the most informative.

**Defects found in the supplement (handle explicitly):**
- 7 "DNA-binding proteins" are actually DNA strands: their sequences contain only A, C, G and T, with lengths 53–147. They are 1AOII, 3THWD, 4FCYC, 4GNXL, 4GNXK, 4JJNJ and 4JJNI.
- 5 negatives contain `X`, although the text says such sequences were removed: 1XMZA, 2C15A, 2V5IA, 2QM0A and 2Y0GA.
- 28 negative IDs have no chain letter (for example 1A8P and 1ABE).
- One line of 2RAUA has a stray "23" from the PDF ("…GEQ / L23VTIS…"). RCSB confirms the true sequence is "…GEQLVTIS…", so strip digits rather than dropping the line.
- 42 PDB186 chain IDs (68 PDB codes) also occur in the benchmark, so the homology filter before the independent test matters.

## 5. Reproduction plan

1. **Parse and clean the benchmark (1 h).** Run `pdftotext -layout` on S1. Split at "(1)." and "(2).", join sequence lines, and strip digits (the 2RAUA artefact). Check 525/550 and 1,075 unique IDs. Record the 7 DNA strands and 5 X-containing sequences. For PDB186, read Lou Dataset S2 (strip CRLF).
2. **PSSMs (shared pipeline; about 8 h of work plus compute).** Run BLAST+ 2.17.0 `psiblast -num_iterations 3 -evalue 0.001 -out_ascii_pssm` for 1,261 sequences. Do two runs: (a) today's nr, which is literal but needs about 800 GB of disk and on the order of 10³ CPU-h; (b) UniRef90 from the 2014_01 archive as a time-matched proxy, which is much faster. Freeze the PSSMs, and store the BLAST version and database date.
3. **PSSM-DT plus SVM jackknife: Table 1 first (lowest risk, most informative).** Compare SN/SP/AUC rather than ACC/MCC (see the consistency check below). Targets: PSSM-DT SN 81.91, SP 78.00, AUC 86.50 (Tables 1–2); PSSM-DDT SN 81.33, SP 78.18; PSSM-SDT SN 77.15, SP 74.93.
4. **Figure 1.** 5-fold CV for LG = 1…10 (or the range on the figure axis), 5 seeds. Target: the optimum at or near LG = 5 and a "modest" effect of LG.
5. **Table 2 and Figure 3.** Implement the 4 alternative encodings. Targets (ACC/MCC/SN/SP/AUC): AvePscore-20 73.95/0.480/68.57/79.09/81.40; AvePscore-400 73.58/0.470/66.47/80.36/81.50; Pscore-100 73.12/0.463/72.76/73.45/80.50; ACC 73.77/0.475/73.14/74.36/81.90. 6. **Table 4 and Figure 5.** Comparator rows: recompute ACC/MCC/SN/SP from Information S1 binary calls, and AUC for DBPPred, DNAbinder and DNABIND from its real-valued outputs. ## 6. Risks and open questions

- **nr drift.** nr has grown about 30-fold since 2014. PSSMs will be more diverse, and DT products of PSSM scores scale with them. Expect shifts of a few ACC points in either direction. The UniRef90-2014_01 run gives a bracket.
- **PSSM transform and feature scaling are unstated.** These are the biggest unknowns after the database. Report all variants tried.
- **Inconsistent headline metrics.** Match SN, SP and AUC, and treat the reported ACC and MCC as unreliable to about ±0.02.
- **Independent-test filter cannot be done as written.** The PDB186 result will depend on the filter chosen. The paper does not give the size of the filtered training set.
- **Contaminated positive set.** The 7 DNA strands among the positives score as near-trivial positives with any profile and may inflate SN. Run with and without them.
- **Comparator reimplementations (Table 3) are the weakest part.** If effort is limited, report only the PSSM-DT, encoding (Table 2) and PDB186 rows.
- **Open question.** Was jackknife C/γ re-tuned per fold? Fixed parameters are assumed.

**Verdict:** Tables 1, 2 and 4 and Figures 1, 3 and 5 can be regenerated approximately in about 4–5 working days after the PSSMs exist, Figure 2 adds about 1 day and Table 3/Figure 4 about 4 more. The comparator rows of Table 4 and three of the six curves in Figure 5 are exact from Lou et al. 2014 Information S1. No item is exactly regenerable end-to-end because of nr drift and the unstated PSSM and feature scaling.
