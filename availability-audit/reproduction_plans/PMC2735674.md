# Reproduction plan: PMC2735674

## 1. Paper

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Title | Supervised prediction of drug–target interactions using bipartite local models |
| Authors | Kevin Bleakley, Yoshihiro Yamanishi |
| Journal | Bioinformatics 25(18):2397–2403 |
| Year | 2009 |
| PMCID | PMC2735674 |
| DOI | 10.1093/bioinformatics/btp433 |
| iCite RCR | 10.435 (paper_catalog.csv; 397 citations, NIH percentile 97.6) |

**Summary.** The paper predicts edges in a drug–target bipartite graph with "bipartite local models" (BLM). For a candidate pair (drug d_i, target t_j), two local SVMs are trained. The first is target-centred: other drugs are labelled by whether they bind t_j, the kernel is the SIMCOMP chemical similarity, and the model scores d_i ("BLMd"). The second is drug-centred: other targets are labelled by whether d_i binds them, the kernel is the normalized Smith–Waterman sequence similarity, and the model scores t_j ("BLMt"). The two SVM decision values are combined with m(x, y) = max(x, y). The method is evaluated by leave-one-out (LOO) on the four Yamanishi et al. (2008) human networks (enzymes, ion channels, GPCRs, nuclear receptors). It is compared with the kernel regression method (KRM) of Yamanishi et al. (2008) and a nearest-neighbour (NN) baseline. **Main claim:** the best BLM-based AUC beats KRM on all four sets (92.9→97.6, 91.7→97.3, 87.3→95.5, 83.6→88.1). AUPR also improves (80.6→84.1, 79.6→81.3, 57.7→66.7, 43.6→61.2). At least 4 of the top-10 new nuclear-receptor predictions were later confirmed.

The paper's own supplement (10×10-fold CV tables, top-20 lists, network images) is not in the PMC OA package; its JATS has no `supplementary-material`.

## 6. Risks and open questions

- **No-positive local models.** This is the biggest unknown, and it covers 43% of NR positives in BLMt. Step 3 calibrates on NR and then tests transfer to the other three sets without retuning. If no single rule fits all four, the BLM rows can only be approximate.
- **KRM numbers may not be LOO.** The asterisked KRM AUCs are "the original AUC results" from Yamanishi 2008, which used 10-fold CV. They may therefore not be reproducible under LOO even with the right hyperparameters.
- **Max over different methods.** Combining KRM and BLM scores with max is sensitive to scale, and the paper does not say whether scores were normalized first.
- **LIBSVM sign convention.** A sign flip would invert the BLM scores; assert that +1 = interacting in unit tests.
- **AUPR definition and ties.** These matter most for NN, where many pairs share the same max-similarity score.
- **"Bold"/asterisk annotations.** These record a 2009 SuperTarget and literature lookup and are not recomputable. Treat them as given labels.
- **Open question.** Was Table 5 ranked by m(BLMd,BLMt) or by the four-way aggregate? Check both; the NR AUPR is nearly equal (60.0 vs 61.2).

**Verdict:** No item is exact. Tables 1–5 and Figs 1–2 are all regenerable approximately in about 4 days. The BLM and NN rows should come close once the no-positive rule is calibrated on NR. The KRM rows depend on a hyperparameter search against the published AUCs.
