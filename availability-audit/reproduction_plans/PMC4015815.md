# Reproduction plan: PMC4015815

**Using distances between Top-n-gram and residue pairs for protein remote homology detection**
Liu B, Xu J, Zou Q, Xu R, Wang X, Chen Q.
*BMC Bioinformatics* 15 (Suppl 2): S3, 2014 (APBC 2014).
PMCID PMC4015815 · PMID 24564580 · DOI [10.1186/1471-2105-15-S2-S3](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-15-S2-S3) · iCite RCR 2.559 (70 citations).

**Summary.** The paper proposes two fixed-length protein representations for SVM-based remote homology detection:

- **SVM-DR** (sequence-based) counts every residue (distance 0) and every ordered residue pair (ti, tj) at each distance d = 1..dMAX. The vector has 20 + 400·dMAX dimensions.
- **SVM-DT** (profile-based) builds the same vector over the Top-1-gram sequence. Each residue is replaced by the most frequent amino acid in its column of a PSI-BLAST frequency profile.

For each of the 54 families of the SCOP 1.53 superfamily benchmark (Liao & Noble; 4,352 sequences, 23 superfamilies), the vectors go into the Gist 2.3 SVM with default parameters, and the families are scored by ROC and ROC50. **Main claim:** with dMAX = 150, SVM-DT reaches average ROC 0.948 and ROC50 0.800, and SVM-DR reaches 0.919 and 0.715. Adding pair distances improves the position-independent Top-n-gram method by 4.1% ROC and 10.4% ROC50. The explicit features can be read: on family 2.5.1.3 the most discriminative pairs are (G,G) and (L,L).

**Related later code, found during this planning: not the lost package, but useful.** The same lab's Pse-in-One 2.0 is at <http://bliulab.net/Pse-in-One2.0/static/download/Pse-in-One-2.0.zip> (163,899,707 bytes; members dated April 2017; downloaded and read 2026-11-02, nothing run). It contains:

- `dr_method` in `nac.py`: DR feature extraction with raw counts. Distance 0 gives residue counts; d = 1..max_dis gives ordered pair counts. This matches the paper's Figure 2.
- `dt_method` in `profile.py`: Top-1-gram, then `dr_method`. Each Top-1-gram is the argmax of columns 23–42 of the PSI-BLAST ASCII PSSM (the weighted observed percentages), ties going to the first amino acid in ARNDCQEGHILKMFPSTWYV order.
- PSI-BLAST settings: `-num_iterations 3 -evalue 0.001`.
- A formatted BLAST database `psiblast/nrdb90/nrdb90`. Its header reads: title "FASTA/nrdb90", built 1 Apr 2016, 534,936 sequences, 178,121,105 residues.

The zip also bundles binaries (psiblast, libsvm, gnuplot) that we did not run. It has no Gist SVM step and no ROC evaluation. The GitHub repository `bioinformatics-hitsz/Pse-in-One` (v1, 2015, commit `bd4f39e3`) has no DR or DT code.

**Data: complete and public.**

- **Benchmark.** <https://noble.gs.washington.edu/proj/svm-pairwise/> is reachable (2026-11-02):
  - `scop.1.53.1e-25.fasta`: 917,584 bytes, 4,352 sequences, lowercase. 238 sequences contain `x`/`X`, and 369 are shorter than 30 residues.
  - `all_seq_fam_membership.txt`: 505,821 bytes, space-separated, 4,352 rows × 54 family columns. Codes: 1 positive train, 2 negative train, 3 positive test, 4 negative test.

**Third-party tools.**

- **PSI-BLAST.** BLAST+ 2.17.0 is current and legacy `blastpgp` 2.2.26 is still downloadable. Either is usable.
- **Gist 2.3.** C source from about 2006, still downloadable. Whether it compiles with today's GCC is unknown. Build and run it only in a sandbox. Failing that, reimplement its objective: normalized linear kernel plus constant, and 2-norm soft margin through the diagonal factor. LIBSVM's default 1-norm C-SVM is not identical.
- **nrdb90.** The EBI release is gone; the lab's 2016-formatted copy ships inside Pse-in-One 2.0.

## Risks and open questions

- **Kernel ambiguity.** The paper says "Gist defaults" (normalized linear), but its direct predecessor used Gist with RBF. If the linear results come out well below 0.919 / 0.948, try RBF too.
- **Profile drift.** We cannot confirm that the Pse-in-One nrdb90 (built 2016) is the database used for this 2014 paper. PSI-BLAST version and iteration count also change the profiles. Expect DT scores to move by about ±0.01 ROC and more on ROC50.
- **Unstated data handling.** Treatment of x/X residues and of the 369 sequences under 30 aa is not given.
- **Gist build.** Gist 2.3 may not compile cleanly today. A substitute solver changes the margins slightly, so DR would then become "approximate" instead of near-exact.
- **Inconsistencies in the paper.** It points to "Figure 2(C) and 2(D)" for what are Figures 5C–D. Its sign wording for Figure 5 contradicts the plot. dMAX "can be any integer between 0 and the longest length", but the sweep grid is only given in the figure.
- **Open question.** Does the 2014 Wayback capture of the DistanceSVM page link to downloadable source with the exact PSI-BLAST settings? This needs checking from a network that can reach web.archive.org.

**Verdict:** The SVM-DR results (the Table 1 DR row, the Figure 3 DR curve and Figures 4B and 5C–D) should come out close to exact in about 2–3 days if Gist 2.3 builds. The SVM-DT results are approximate, because the original profile database is gone; they need about 1 more day plus roughly a day of PSI-BLAST compute on the 2016 nrdb90. Figures 1–2 are not results.
