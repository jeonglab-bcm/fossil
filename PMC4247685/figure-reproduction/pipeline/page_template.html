Curry 2014BMC Bioinformatics 15:355 GABi In the overlay, ink is theirs and green is ours

What we matched

Figs 3 to 5 are base R graphics and Fig. 2 is ggplot2. We drew each one with the same tools and mapped it onto the published figure’s pixel grid using its plot frame alone. The curves inside the frame are compared, not fitted.

Figs 3 to 5 device
Each published frame is exactly the plot region of R’s default png() device: 480×480 px at 72 dpi, pointsize 12, default margins, giving 391×347.5 px. The images are cropped to that frame and enlarged 2.00× (Fig. 3), 2.01× (Fig. 4) and 2.14× (Fig. 5). We draw on that device and enlarge by the same frame-to-frame scale with bicubic resampling.
Survival curves
plot(survfit(...), lty = c(1, 2), mark.time = TRUE) with xaxs = "S", the survival 2.x default (the axis starts at 0). survival 3 changed the default to “r”.
Fig. 2 theme
The published text widths fit ggplot2 0.9.3’s theme sizes at a base of 8.6 pt: axis titles at 8.6 pt, tick labels, strips and legend at 0.8× that. The theme has white strips and panels with black borders, grey50 tick labels, no x tick labels and a bold “Method”. Tick length, legend key size and gaps were measured in pixels.
Alignment
One scale and offset per axis, taken from the frame lines (the facet grid in Fig. 2). Nothing inside the frame is used for fitting.
Reading the views
Overlay: ink is the paper only, light green is the reproduction only, dark green is both. Difference: darker means a larger grey-level difference (×2). Mean |Δ| includes JPEG artefacts in the published images.
