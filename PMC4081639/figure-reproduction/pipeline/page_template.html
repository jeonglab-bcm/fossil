What we matched

Each reproduction is drawn with ggplot2 through tikzDevice and pdfLaTeX, the toolchain the paper’s typography points to. It is styled with ggplot2’s 2013 defaults and rendered at the published figure’s exact pixel size, so the two images can be laid over each other directly.

Package and theme
ggplot2 with the 0.9.3 theme_grey defaults restated: grey panel, white major and lighter minor gridlines, grey50 axis text at 0.8×, 0.15 cm ticks, grey95 legend keys of 1.2 lines, grey80 strips.
Fonts
tikzDevice, then pdfLaTeX, in Computer Modern, the LaTeX default, with ψ, n, μ and 10−14 set in math mode as in the paper. Times scored within a few percent on stroke alignment, so at this resolution the typeface can’t be identified with certainty.
Lines
72.27/96 pt per lwd (R’s own convention) instead of current tikzDevice’s 0.4 pt, which matches the published lines of about 1 pt. Dashes are 2 pt on, 2 pt off, as measured in Fig. 4.
Colours
Panel and strip fills sampled from the published JPEGs. These read lighter than ggplot’s grey90 (#E5E5E5): #E9E9E9 in Figs 2 and 6, #EEEEEE in Figs 4 and 5. Strips read #D4D4D4 rather than grey80 (#CCCCCC). Text keeps ggplot’s black and grey50.
Size
Drawing size and raster resolution fitted per figure so the panel lands on the published pixels, then cropped to the published frame (538×521, 565×471, 570×473 and 586×393 px).
Spacing
Axis-title gaps, legend position and y-title sizes tuned per figure by local alignment. ggplot2 0.9.3 laid out title rows differently from 4.x, and the published y-titles measure 9.9 to 12.7 pt rather than a uniform 12 pt.
Reading the views
Overlay: ink is the paper only, light green is the reproduction only, dark green is both. Difference: darker means a larger grey-level difference (×2). Mean |Δ| includes JPEG artefacts in the published images.
